# Taaseel Method

## Overview

Taaseel is Mohamed's separate full-journey method for explanation, research, and thinking.

It does not replace Mohamed's older methods:

- Batariqatna: structured explanation, terms, tables, and examples.
- Falsafa: why before how, story, small example, law, and golden sentence.

Taaseel is a third method. Use it when Mohamed wants the full path from origin to application.

The core rule is:

Why before how.

Never start with a definition, formula, or procedure when the task requires understanding. Start with the reason the idea exists.

## When to Use

Use this skill when Mohamed asks for:

- شرح عميق.
- بحث وتحليل.
- طريقة تفكير.

- شرح مالي أو محاسبي أو إداري أو تقني.
- إنتاج محتوى تعليمي بأسلوب محمد.

Do not use the full method for tiny factual answers. Use the short version instead.

## Method Catalog Rule

Mohamed has three separate explanation methods:

- Batariqatna: use when he wants clean structured explanation with definitions, sections, tables, and examples.
- Falsafa: use when he wants deep origin logic, why before how, a simple story, a small example, law extraction, and a golden sentence.
- Taaseel: use when he wants the full journey: question, story, law, structured explanation, proof example, and executive summary.

Do not collapse these methods into one. If Mohamed names a method, follow that method exactly.

## The Six Stages

### 1. Open the Door

Start with the real question, not the definition.

Ask:

- Why does this topic exist?
- What problem does it solve?
- What decision does it help with?
- What would go wrong if we misunderstood it?

Output: one central question that makes the topic necessary.

### 2. Build the Meaning

Turn the concept into a scene, relation, or story.

Ask:

- What moves?
- What changes?
- What stays fixed?
- What affects what?
- What are we really measuring?

Output: a living explanation of the inner logic.

### 3. Extract the Law

Derive the rule from the scene.

The formula, definition, or principle should feel like a conclusion, not a burden.

Ask:

- What rule naturally appears from the meaning?
- When does it work?
- When does it fail?
- What is the one golden sentence?

Output: a clear rule plus a memorable golden sentence.

### 4. Engineer the Explanation

Organize what is already understood.

Use:

- General idea.
- Core terms.
- Logical divisions.
- Details.
- Relationships.
- Examples.
- Common mistakes.
- Practical summary.

Rule: structure comes after understanding, not before it.

### 5. Fix the Understanding

Stabilize the idea with a practical proof.

Use one or more:

- Numeric example.
- Comparison.
- Small table.
- Common mistake and correction.
- Real case.
- Review question.

Output: evidence that the explanation works outside abstract prose.

### 6. Convert to Execution

End with use, not admiration.

Ask:

- When should Mohamed use this?
- What signal should trigger it?
- What decision changes because of it?
- What mistake does it prevent?
- What is the next action?

Output: one-line summary, practical use, warning, and next question or decision.

## Maturity Tests

A Taaseel explanation is not mature until it passes three tests:

1. It can be compressed into a one-minute explanation after completion.
2. It can be represented by a small example.
3. It can state when not to use the concept.

If any test fails, the explanation is still immature. Refine the meaning, example, or boundaries before delivering.

## Short Version

For quick answers, use:

1. The real question.
2. The meaning.
3. The summary.
4. The use.

## Quality Rules

- Meaning before terminology.
- Question before answer.
- Derivation before memorization.
- Structure after understanding.
- Use before admiration.
- Every explanation ends with a practical decision or application.

## Output Shape

Every full Taaseel output should contain this fixed movement:

- A question that opens the door.
- A story or scene that builds meaning.
- A law that emerges from logic.
- A complete organized explanation.
- A small example that proves the idea.
- An executive summary that turns understanding into use.

## Mohamed-Specific Handling

When writing for Mohamed:

- Arabic first.
- Do not mix English into Arabic lines.
- Keep explanations warm, practical, and direct.
- Tie the output to Mohamed's systems when useful: investing, Hermes, Obsidian vault, books, media notes, or business ideas.
- Do not turn every idea into a new project.
- Classify the result when relevant: act now, save, attach to project, research more, or park.

## Verification Checklist

Before finishing:

- The output starts with why, not a dry definition.
- The central question is explicit.
- The meaning is built through relations or a scene.
- The rule is derived, not dumped.
- There is at least one example or comparison.
- There is a practical use.
- There is a warning or common mistake.
- The output passes the one-minute explanation test.
- The output includes or can produce a small example.
- The output states when not to use the concept.
- If research was used, source claims, added research, interpretation, and uncertainty are distinguishable.
- If the topic is high-stakes, the output includes a critique or limitation layer.
- If relevant, the output classifies the next step: act now, save, attach to project, research more, or park.
- The next decision or question is clear.
