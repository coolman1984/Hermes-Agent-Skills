# Accounting and Management Concepts with Taaseel

Use this reference when Mohamed asks for Taaseel on accounting, cost, finance, operations, pricing, or management concepts.

## Purpose

The goal is not to define the term first. The goal is to make Mohamed own the managerial reason behind the concept.

Start from the business problem, then derive the rule or formula naturally.

## Preferred Movement

1. Translate the English term into a clear Arabic operational name when needed.
2. Open with the problem the concept exists to solve.
3. Build a small business scene: restaurant, factory, product, campaign, department, or project.
4. Identify what was planned, what happened, and what decision is needed.
5. Derive the rule from the scene instead of dumping a definition.
6. Separate similar concepts clearly, especially when one is often confused with another.
7. Add a small numeric example.
8. Explain when the concept should not be applied rigidly.
9. End with a practical question Mohamed can use in real work.

## Cost and Control Concepts

For cost-control topics, make this distinction explicit:

- Cost control is not blind cost cutting.
- Cost control compares planned cost, actual cost, reasons for variance, responsibility, and corrective action.
- Cost reduction asks whether the same result can be achieved at lower cost.
- Blind cutting can harm quality, customer trust, growth, or learning.

## Practical Example Shape

Use very small numbers.

Example pattern:

- planned unit cost
- actual unit cost
- total planned cost
- total actual cost
- variance
- variance percentage
- possible causes
- decision

If doing arithmetic, calculate with a tool before writing the final answer.

## Questions to Ask Internally

- What leak is the concept trying to reveal?
- What decision does it improve?
- What would management do differently after understanding it?
- Who owns the cause of the variance or issue?
- Is this a control problem, a planning problem, a pricing problem, or a learning-stage problem?

## Maturity Tests for This Class

Before delivering, make sure the explanation can answer:

- Can Mohamed explain it in one minute?
- Can it be shown with a tiny example?
- Can he say when not to use it rigidly?
- Can he distinguish it from the nearest confused concept?

## Common Pitfall

Do not make accounting and management concepts feel like dictionary definitions.

For Mohamed, they should feel like decision tools.
