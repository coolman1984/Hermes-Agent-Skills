# Research Enrichment for Taaseel

Use this reference when Mohamed asks for Taaseel on a topic that needs research, source analysis, or high-stakes understanding.

This borrows useful operating patterns from Mohamed's media-drop workflow, but Taaseel remains a separate method with a separate purpose.

## Boundary Rule

Do not turn Taaseel into a media-note workflow.

Media-drop handles links, transcripts, extraction, Downloads files, and media-source bridges.

Taaseel handles explanation, research, thinking, teaching, and concept ownership.

Borrow the useful research discipline only.

## Useful Patterns Borrowed

### 1. Source Brief Before Explanation

Before writing, build a small source brief when sources exist:

- direct claims
- examples
- definitions
- names and institutions
- dates and sequence
- links and references
- open questions
- what seems missing

### 2. Depth Selection

Choose depth before writing:

- Quick: when Mohamed asks for speed or a small answer.
- Standard: default for normal explanation and research.
- Deep: money, investment, law, health, reputation, security, or active Mohamed systems.
- Parked: interesting but low leverage.

Do not over-research every topic.

### 3. Provenance Separation

When research is involved, distinguish:

- what the source says
- what external research adds
- Mohamed-facing interpretation
- what remains uncertain

Arabic final outputs may use these labels:

- من المصدر مباشرة
- من البحث الإضافي
- قراءة عملية لمحمد
- ما يزال غير مؤكد

Use labels only when helpful. Do not force them into tiny explanations.

### 4. Big Questions

A researched Taaseel explanation should be able to answer:

- What is the core claim?
- Why does it matter now?
- Who benefits or loses?
- How does the mechanism work?
- When did the key shift happen?
- Where is the action concentrated?
- What philosophy or incentive sits underneath?
- What is missing or weak?
- What would a skeptical expert object to?
- What should Mohamed do next?

### 5. Source Quality Ladder

Prefer:

1. Primary source, official documentation, paper, law, dataset, filing, transcript.
2. Expert or institutional source.
3. Serious journalism with evidence and links.
4. Specialist blogs or technical writeups.
5. Commentary and social posts.

If the evidence is weak, say so.

### 6. Critique Layer

Do not just explain elegantly. Challenge the idea:

- omitted context
- weak evidence
- competing explanations
- overstated certainty
- old numbers
- emotional framing
- correlation versus causation

### 7. Mohamed Application Layer

Translate the concept into Mohamed's systems:

- investment decisions
- Hermes and agent workflows
- Obsidian vault
- media notes
- book factory
- business ideas
- company dashboard and reporting systems

Then classify:

- act now
- save
- attach to an existing project
- research more
- park

### 8. Verification Discipline

Before delivering a researched Taaseel output, check:

- central question is explicit
- source and interpretation are not blended carelessly
- limitations are visible
- practical use is clear
- maturity tests pass
- no new project is created by accident

## Anti-Bloat Rule

Research should deepen the explanation, not bury Mohamed under references.

If the topic becomes too large, deliver a useful Taaseel explanation and create a separate research-needed note or next-step question.
