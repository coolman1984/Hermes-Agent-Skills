# Reasoning Under Uncertainty and Rigor

Separate knowns, assumptions, unknowns. Use confidence levels, Bayesian updating, base rates, steelman opposing views, red-team your conclusion, and identify falsifying evidence.

Primary sources: Kahneman & Tversky, “Judgment under Uncertainty: Heuristics and Biases” (Science, 1974), DOI 10.1126/science.185.4157.1124; Kahneman, Thinking, Fast and Slow.

Procedure:
1. What do we know from evidence?
2. What are we assuming?
3. What would change our mind?
4. Base rate?
5. Confidence and downside if wrong.
6. Red-team: best argument against chosen answer.

Invoke this when: evidence is incomplete and stakes matter. This misleads when: you invent fake probabilities.
